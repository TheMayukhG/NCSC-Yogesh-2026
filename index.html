01 / THE PLANT

Easy to overlook.
Difficult to ignore.

Leucas aspera is an erect, diffusely branched annual herb belonging to the Lamiaceae family. Its small white flowers appear in dense whorls around the stem, while its narrow leaves give the plant a distinctly rough appearance.

It is commonly encountered in open areas, roadsides, fallow fields and other disturbed habitats.

“A familiar plant can hold an unfamiliar amount of knowledge.”

03 / MORPHOLOGY

Look closer.

The easiest way to recognise Leucas aspera is to look at its combination of hairy leaves, angular stem and compact white flower whorls.

01 / STEM

Stem

Upright, branched and characteristically hairy, giving the plant a rough texture.

02 / LEAVES

Leaves

Narrow, linear to oblong leaves occur in opposite pairs along the stem.

03 / FLOWER

Flower

Small white flowers form dense, rounded whorls around the stem.

04 / FRUIT

Fruit

The fruit consists of small nutlets, characteristic of the mint family.

04 / HABITAT

Where the wild herb belongs.

In India, the plant can be found in plains, roadsides, fallow fields, wastelands and other open habitats.

A plant is not only a biological object.

It can also be a piece of cultural memory.
01

Traditional names

The plant is known by different vernacular names across regions, including Thumba/Thumbai and Sanskrit Dronapushpi.

02

Traditional medicine

Various traditional systems describe medicinal applications for the plant. Such traditional uses should be distinguished from clinically proven treatments.

03

Ethnobotanical knowledge

Knowledge about plants can be passed through families and communities, making documentation an important part of cultural conservation.

05 / SCIENCE

What does science actually tell us?

Scientific literature has investigated phytochemicals and biological activities associated with Leucas aspera. Evidence should be presented carefully, without turning traditional claims into unsupported medical promises.

PHYTOCHEMISTRY

Plant compounds

Published studies have reported various phytochemical constituents from different parts of the plant.

RESEARCH

Biological activity

Laboratory research has investigated several biological activities of extracts and isolated compounds.

IMPORTANT

Evidence matters

Traditional medicinal use and laboratory findings do not by themselves establish that a plant can cure a disease.
